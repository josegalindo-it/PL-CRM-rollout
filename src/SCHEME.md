# Holding vendors accountable on a global CRM rollout — without configuring a single platform myself.

> A proposal for the Senior Project Manager / CIO delivery lead role: vendor governance and integrated programme discipline across a multi-country, multi-vendor Veeva/Salesforce rollout for a life-sciences commercial organization.

## Motivation

### The role this brief describes is a governance role, not a platform role — and that's exactly my lane.

The brief is explicit on this point: "you will not be configuring the platforms yourself... your job is to make sure the people who do deliver on time, on budget and to the standard the business needs." That is precisely the kind of engagement I look for — being the single point of coordination across several vendors and implementation partners working in parallel, holding them to their statements of work, and keeping business and IT stakeholders aligned through a multi-country rollout. I want to be upfront about one thing: I do not have prior life-sciences or Veeva/Salesforce-specific delivery experience. What I bring instead is the same vendor-accountability and programme-governance discipline this role is built around, applied previously at comparable scale in other regulated, multi-vendor environments.

## What I bring

### Twenty years of vendor accountability and programme governance at enterprise scale.

- **Multi-vendor P&L and executive governance:** acted as executive single point of contact for a €6.5M/year, 75-professional multi-vendor IT account — the same vendor-accountability model this role's CIO delivery lead mandate calls for.
- **Enterprise programme governance:** directed single-program PMO governance exceeding 200,000 delivery hours (215,000 in the largest), and more than 500,000 hours cumulative across enterprise programs — the scale of integrated planning, steering and executive reporting a global, multi-vendor rollout needs.
- **Managed-services and SLA oversight:** currently governs a managed-services environment above 130,000 hours/year, holding delivery partners accountable to service levels and escalation paths — the same discipline vendor supervision demands here.
- **Delivery under regulatory compliance:** delivered RDA-BI/BCBS 239, a regulated cross-team program (58,000 hours) for a Tier-1 financial institution — direct experience operating under strict compliance and audit requirements, the same posture a GDPR/HCP-transparency-bound rollout needs, even though the regulated domain differs.

PMP® and ITIL® 4 Foundation certified.

## Project · Proposal {nav="Project"}

### How I'd structure vendor and programme governance for this rollout.

#### The problem

Rolling out CRM capabilities (Veeva, Salesforce, or a combination) to a commercial organization across multiple markets, through several vendors and implementation partners working in parallel, alongside global and local business teams. The exposure is not platform configuration itself — that sits with the platform providers and systems integrators — but delivery and governance risk around it: uncoordinated vendor dependencies and handovers, local markets drifting from the global template without challenge, and market-readiness gaps (data migration, training, compliance sign-off) surfacing only at go-live.

#### The proposed solution

A standardized vendor-governance and programme-control methodology, not dependent on prior Veeva/Salesforce or life-sciences-specific experience: a single point of contact across all vendors, structured SOW/milestone/SLA oversight, steering committees and working groups with explicit decision rights, and a market-readiness gating model that sequences data migration, local configuration, training and compliance sign-off before every go-live. Platform-specific fluency and life-sciences commercial context are built in direct coordination with the platform vendors' functional leads and the client's business/compliance teams, without that gap sitting on the programme's critical path.

#### Conceptual architecture

*vendor and programme governance for the rollout — not Veeva/Salesforce platform configuration, which sits with the platform providers and systems integrators*

- **Vendor & SOW governance:** single point of contact across all vendors (platform providers, systems integrators, data providers, content agencies); milestones, deliverables and SLAs tracked and escalated against each statement of work.
- **Cadence:** steering committees, vendor boards and cross-vendor working groups, each with explicit decision rights.
- **Programme control:** RAID log, budget tracking and status reporting maintained for the CIO and executive sponsors; global template vs. local deviation tracked and challenged explicitly, not left to drift.
- **Market readiness gates:** data migration, local configuration, training, compliance sign-off and hypercare sequenced and gated per market before go-live.
- **Boundary, stated explicitly:** this architecture governs vendor delivery, programme control and market readiness. It does not extend into Veeva/Salesforce platform configuration or life-sciences commercial strategy, which sit with the platform providers/systems integrators and the client's business teams respectively.

## Plan & Risks {nav="Plan"}

### Governance in place from week one, not discovered at the first missed handover.

#### First week

- **Vendor & stakeholder mapping:** meet the CIO, the business/IT/commercial/compliance stakeholders and every vendor (platform providers, systems integrators, data providers, content agencies) to confirm current SOWs, milestones and the state of the integrated plan.
- **Governance cadence stood up:** launch the RAID log, budget tracker and status reporting, and set the steering committee, vendor board and working-group cadence with explicit decision rights, from day one.
- **Market-readiness baseline:** confirm the data migration/local configuration/training/compliance sign-off/hypercare sequencing already defined per market, and flag any gaps against the global template.

#### Risks

- **Vendor dependency and handover gaps:** hold a single integrated plan across all vendors with cross-vendor dependencies and handovers tracked explicitly, escalated at the vendor board rather than discovered at go-live.
- **Local markets drifting from the global template without governance:** apply a documented process to challenge and approve local deviations, keeping global-vs-local trade-offs visible to the steering committee instead of resolved informally.
- **Compliance or readiness slippage at market go-live:** gate every market's go-live behind an explicit readiness checklist (data migration, training, compliance sign-off) — the same cutover-gating discipline applied on prior regulated, multi-vendor programs.
