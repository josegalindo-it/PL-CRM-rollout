Project · Proposal

How I'd structure vendor and programme governance for this rollout.

The problem

Rolling out CRM capabilities (Veeva, Salesforce, or a combination) to a commercial organization across multiple markets, through several vendors and implementation partners working in parallel, alongside global and local business teams. The exposure is not platform configuration itself — that sits with the platform providers and systems integrators — but delivery and governance risk around it: uncoordinated vendor dependencies and handovers, local markets drifting from the global template without challenge, and market-readiness gaps (data migration, training, compliance sign-off) surfacing only at go-live.

The proposed solution

A standardized vendor-governance and programme-control methodology, not dependent on prior Veeva/Salesforce or life-sciences-specific experience: a single point of contact across all vendors, structured SOW/milestone/SLA oversight, steering committees and working groups with explicit decision rights, and a market-readiness gating model that sequences data migration, local configuration, training and compliance sign-off before every go-live. Platform-specific fluency and life-sciences commercial context are built in direct coordination with the platform vendors' functional leads and the client's business/compliance teams, without that gap sitting on the programme's critical path.

Plan & Risks

Governance in place from week one, not discovered at the first missed handover.

First week

  • Vendor & stakeholder mapping: meet the CIO, the business/IT/commercial/compliance stakeholders and every vendor (platform providers, systems integrators, data providers, content agencies) to confirm current SOWs, milestones and the state of the integrated plan.
  • Governance cadence stood up: launch the RAID log, budget tracker and status reporting, and set the steering committee, vendor board and working-group cadence with explicit decision rights, from day one.
  • Market-readiness baseline: confirm the data migration/local configuration/training/compliance sign-off/hypercare sequencing already defined per market, and flag any gaps against the global template.

Risks

  • Vendor dependency and handover gaps: hold a single integrated plan across all vendors with cross-vendor dependencies and handovers tracked explicitly, escalated at the vendor board rather than discovered at go-live.
  • Local markets drifting from the global template without governance: apply a documented process to challenge and approve local deviations, keeping global-vs-local trade-offs visible to the steering committee instead of resolved informally.
  • Compliance or readiness slippage at market go-live: gate every market's go-live behind an explicit readiness checklist (data migration, training, compliance sign-off) — the same cutover-gating discipline applied on prior regulated, multi-vendor programs.
